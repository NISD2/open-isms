## Purpose

Produce consistent, high-quality YouTube videos efficiently — from script to published — without a production team. Each video serves two functions: direct SEO value on YouTube, and content that can be clipped for LinkedIn and Twitter.

---

## System Overview

```
INPUT: Script (approved, timed)
   ↓
PHASE 1: Setup (one-time, ~2 hours)
   ↓
PHASE 2: Practice (30 min per video)
   ↓
PHASE 3: Filming (30–60 min per video)
   ↓
PHASE 4: Editing (60–90 min per video)
   ↓
PHASE 5: Upload & SEO (20 min per video)
   ↓
PHASE 6: Promotion (15 min per video)
   ↓
OUTPUT: Published video + LinkedIn clip + thumbnail
```

## PHASE 0 — One-Time Setup

Do this once before filming the first video. Revisit if you change location.

### Camera

- **Recommended:** iPhone 13 or newer (excellent quality, no cost)
- Settings: 1080p at 30fps (4K is unnecessary and harder to edit)
- Mount on a tripod at eye level — camera slightly above eye line looks best
- Distance from camera: 60–90cm. Fill the top two-thirds of the frame with your face and shoulders.
- **Do not:** hold the phone, prop it against something unstable, or shoot portrait (vertical) for YouTube

### Lighting

**Option A — Natural light (free, best result):**
- Sit facing a window. The window should be in front of you, not behind you.
- Overcast daylight is ideal. Direct sunlight creates harsh shadows.
- Film between 9am–3pm for consistent natural light.

**Option B — Artificial (if natural isn't available):**
- One key light at roughly 45 degrees to your left or right, slightly above eye level
- Elgato Key Light or any LED panel with adjustable colour temperature (~€80–120)
- Set to 5500K (daylight) for a clean, neutral look
- Avoid ring lights directly in front — they flatten the face

**What to avoid:**
- Window or bright light behind you (silhouette)
- Overhead ceiling light as the only source (unflattering, shadows under eyes)
- Mixed light temperatures (warm bulb + cool daylight = colour cast)

### Audio

Audio matters more than video quality. A slightly soft image is fine. Bad audio loses viewers immediately.

**Recommended options (in order of preference):**

| Option | Device | Cost | Notes |
|--------|--------|------|-------|
| Desk mic | Rode NT-USB Mini or Blue Yeti | €80–150 | Best quality, stays on desk |
| Lapel mic | Rode Wireless GO II | €280 | Wireless, no cable in shot |
| Shotgun mic | Rode VideoMicro (on phone) | €60 | Attaches to phone |
| Built-in phone mic | — | Free | Acceptable if within 50cm |

**Room treatment:**
- Soft furnishings (sofa, bookshelf, curtains) absorb echo
- Hard empty rooms echo — avoid or add soft items
- Record a 10-second silent clip and listen back before filming. If you hear hum or echo — fix it before recording.

### Background

**Goal:** Professional and human. Not corporate, not messy.

**Good options:**
- A bookshelf with a curated selection of books (authenticity signal)
- A clean wall with one or two framed items — nothing distracting
- A simple desk setup — monitor, plant, nothing cluttered
- Slight depth-of-field blur if your camera supports it (portrait mode)

**Avoid:**
- White blank walls (cheap and flat)
- Busy backgrounds where viewers read text instead of listening
- Anything company-branded directly behind you (looks like a news set)
- Beds, kitchens, or obviously personal spaces

### Test Recording Checklist (one-time + before each video)

- [ ] Camera: eye level, fills top 2/3 of frame, stable
- [ ] Lighting: face is lit evenly, no harsh shadows, no blown-out background
- [ ] Audio: record 10 seconds of silence, listen back — no hum, no echo
- [ ] Background: clean, nothing distracting visible
- [ ] Frame: enough headroom, shoulders visible, centred
- [ ] Phone: Do Not Disturb mode ON, notifications off, enough storage

## PHASE 1 — Script Preparation

**Time required:** 20 minutes
**Input:** Approved script from `videos/scripts/`

1. Read the script aloud once, timing yourself
   - Target: 4:30–5:30 for a 5-minute video
   - If over 6 minutes: cut. If under 4 minutes: add one more concrete example
2. Mark the 4–5 key points you must cover (not word-for-word lines)
3. Put the script away. You are not memorising it — you are internalising the structure.
4. Read the key points once more. That's your preparation.

**Why not read from a script or teleprompter?**
Reading sounds like reading. The goal is a knowledgeable friend talking directly to you — not a presenter delivering slides. Natural stumbles and rephrasing are fine. They are more trustworthy than polished delivery.

---

## PHASE 2 — Practice Take

**Time required:** 15–20 minutes

1. Set up camera and hit record
2. Do one full practice run from memory using only the key points
3. Watch it back (the full thing — don't skip)
4. Note: any section you stumbled badly on? Any part that was too long? Any phrase that sounded unnatural?
5. Do one more pass on the sections that need work only — not the whole video again
6. **Done.** Don't over-rehearse. The first few takes are usually the best.

## PHASE 3 — Filming

**Time required:** 30–60 minutes

### Before hitting record
- [ ] Test recording checklist complete (see Phase 0)
- [ ] Phone on Do Not Disturb
- [ ] Water within reach but out of frame
- [ ] Script visible nearby for reference if needed — but don't read from it

### Filming approach
- Aim for 2–3 full takes. Pick the best one.
- If you stumble badly: pause, breathe, restart the sentence — don't restart the whole video
- Look directly at the camera lens, not at your own face on the screen
- Speak slightly slower than feels natural on camera — it lands better than you think
- End each take with 5 seconds of silence before stopping the recording (useful in editing)

### What makes a good take
- You look comfortable, not stiff
- You're talking TO someone, not delivering TO a camera
- Natural pauses between sections — these are good, not dead air
- You don't sound like you're reading

### After filming
- Do NOT delete any takes yet
- Review takes quickly (1.5x speed) and mark the best one
- Note any sections from other takes that were better than the chosen one

## PHASE 4 — Editing

**Time required:** 60–90 minutes
**Software options:**
- **DaVinci Resolve** (free, professional, Mac/Windows/Linux)
- **iMovie** (free, Mac only, easier for beginners)
- **CapCut** (free, simple, also good for creating short clips)

### Editing checklist

**Structure:**
- [ ] Cut the dead air at the start and end of the recording
- [ ] Remove any false starts or extended stumbles (keep natural ones — they're fine)
- [ ] Remove any filler-heavy sections that drag (watch at 1.5x to spot these)
- [ ] Check total length: aim for 5:00–5:30. Anything over 6:00 needs cuts.

**Audio:**
- [ ] Normalise audio levels (in DaVinci: right-click clip → Normalise Audio)
- [ ] Remove background hum if present (DaVinci has a Noise Reduction effect)
- [ ] No music needed for a direct-to-camera explainer — it often competes with speech

**Visual:**
- [ ] Colour correction if needed (most phone footage needs minimal adjustment)
- [ ] Add captions/subtitles — required for LinkedIn, strongly recommended for YouTube
  - YouTube auto-generates these — download them, fix errors, re-upload
  - Or use CapCut's auto-caption feature before exporting
- [ ] No intro animation, no lower-third name cards needed for the first 10 videos — add these later if it makes sense

**Export settings:**
- 1080p, H.264, 30fps
- File size: aim for under 1GB for easy upload

### Thumbnail creation

Do this immediately after editing while the video is fresh.

**Tools:** Canva (free tier is fine) or Adobe Express

**Thumbnail formula:**
- Your face (high energy, not neutral)
- Large bold text: "Was ist NIS2?" or "What is NIS2?"
- Brand colour background (#284b63 navy) or clean white
- No more than 5 words on the thumbnail
- Test: does it read clearly at 120×68px (thumbnail size in YouTube search)? If not, simplify.

**Size:** 1280×720px, JPG or PNG

## PHASE 5 — Upload & SEO

**Time required:** 20 minutes

### Upload checklist

- [ ] Upload video file (MP4, 1080p)
- [ ] Upload custom thumbnail
- [ ] Upload corrected subtitle file (.srt) if you have it

### Title
- Include the main keyword in the first 5 words
- German: *Was ist NIS2? Einfach erklärt in 5 Minuten*
- English: *What is NIS2? Explained Simply in 5 Minutes*
- Max 60 characters

### Description
- First 2 lines (before "Show more") are most important for SEO — put the keyword and value proposition here
- Include timestamps for each section (even for short videos — YouTube rewards this)
- Include link to nisd2.eu in the first paragraph
- Template:

```
[What the video covers in 1-2 sentences. Include primary keyword.]

Timestamps:
0:00 Intro
0:20 What is NIS2?
1:10 Are you affected?
2:10 What does it require?
3:30 What to do next
4:20 Where to start

🔗 Free NIS2 applicability check: nisd2.eu
📩 Questions? Comment below or reach us at contact@nisd2.eu

[3-5 sentences of additional context for SEO]
```

### Tags (paste these)

**German video:**
NIS2, NIS2 Deutschland, NIS2 erklärt, BSIG, NIS2 Pflichten, NIS2 Mittelstand, BSI Registrierung, Cybersicherheit Unternehmen, NIS2 2025, NIS2 Betroffenheit

**English video:**
NIS2, NIS2 explained, NIS2 compliance, NIS2 Germany, BSIG Germany, EU cybersecurity directive, cybersecurity compliance 2025, NIS2 Mittelstand, BSI Germany, NIS2 requirements

### Settings
- Visibility: Public
- Category: Education
- Comments: On (reply to every comment in the first 48 hours — YouTube rewards engagement)
- Language: German (DE) or English (EN) — set this correctly

## PHASE 6 — Promotion

**Time required:** 15 minutes
**Do this the same day the video goes live.**

- [ ] Post on LinkedIn referencing the video (use the relevant awareness post from the content batches as a companion, or write a 3-line post with the link)
- [ ] Post on Twitter/X with the video link and one key insight from it
- [ ] Reply to all comments within 48 hours of posting — YouTube's algorithm rewards this heavily in the first 48 hours
- [ ] If you have an email list: include the video in the next send

### LinkedIn companion post format

```
I made a short video explaining [topic] — because I kept getting asked the same question.

[One sentence on what the video covers.]

[One insight from the video that stands alone.]

Full video: [YouTube link]
```

## Feedback Loop

After each video:
- Note what took longer than expected
- Log the first 48-hour view and comment count
- Note any comments that suggest follow-up video topics
- If a video gets unusual engagement — understand why and replicate

After 5 videos:
- Review average view duration (YouTube Studio → Content)
- If drop-off is early in the video: the hook needs work
- If drop-off is mid-video: that section is too long
- Adjust the script template accordingly
