# Lesson 3.1: The Questions Every CEO Must Answer

If your regulator walked into your office tomorrow and asked about your cybersecurity, how many questions could you answer without turning to your [[CISO]]? The German regulator has published a list of structured questions every management body must be able to answer. This lesson is that list.

## What the rule requires

Article 20(2) of the NIS2 Directive states: "Member States shall ensure that the members of the management bodies of essential and important entities are required to follow training." Every CEO in a covered company must be trained well enough to understand what risks the business faces and what the company is doing about them.

The BSI's guidance for management training (the Handreichung, version 1.0 of 17 April 2026) describes what can be expected of you after training. These questions are a concrete practical test of Article 20(2), published by the BSI.

On 28 October 2026 the BSI followed up with a curriculum for management training, with its own guiding questions for each of its six modules. The questions that go beyond the list below are covered in these lessons: 1.2 (what sits inside the scope), 1.5 (portfolio responsibility on the board), 1.11 (the reporting deadline at night and at weekends), 2.1 and 2.3 (the biggest risks and consciously accepted residual risks) and 5.1 (which decisions follow the training).

## The questions

The BSI organises its test into ten areas - five foundation questions, one area with ten sub-questions on the mandatory measures, and four deeper checks.

**Foundation:**
1. Do I know whether my company falls under NIS2 and understand the [[scope]] of the law?
2. Do our [[risk-management measures]] meet the law's requirements, and are they documented?
3. Can I classify a [[significant incident]] correctly and report it on time?
4. Are we registered correctly with the regulator, and do we keep that registration current?
5. Do I understand my personal duties, training requirements, liability, and the sanctions I face?

**The ten mandatory measures:**
6.1 Is our risk analysis up to date and aligned with current threats?
6.2 Do we have a tested incident response plan?
6.3 Can we recover from backup, and is our crisis management tested?
6.4 Is supplier security checked and actively managed?
6.5 Is security built into how we acquire, develop, and maintain systems?
6.6 Do we measure the effectiveness of our security controls?
6.7 Do we run cyber hygiene programmes and staff training?
6.8 Do we use cryptography and encryption where required?
6.9 Do we manage personnel security, access control, and asset management?
6.10 Do we use multi-factor authentication and secured communications?

**Deeper checks:**
7. Does our risk analysis cover all relevant assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and types of damage - including reputational and regulatory harm?
8. Do we evaluate how each risk affects the availability, integrity, and confidentiality of our services, as well as our financial stability?
9. Do we account for sector-specific requirements and the business processes that matter most?
10. Do we run scenario exercises, and have I personally participated in one?

## What to do with this list

Print these questions and go through them with your CISO, rating each as: confident, with help, or cannot answer. Every question in the third column is a [[gap]] your training must close. Your [[auditor|audit]]or or consultant will call this list the BSI [[Leitfragen]].

## Key takeaways

1. The BSI expects that after training you can answer around twenty concrete questions about your own cybersecurity - ten areas, including ten drill-down questions on the mandatory measures.
2. The questions are not legally binding. They are the published [[baseline]] the BSI uses to describe what can be expected of you after training in the sense of Article 20(2); its curriculum of October 2026 adds guiding questions per module.
3. If you cannot answer one of them today, that is the exact gap your training has to close.
