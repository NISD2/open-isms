# Lesson 1.7: Personal Liability Under Your Country's Corporate Law

Your company gets fined ten million euros for a NIS2 failure. Your [[supervisory board]] meets the next morning. The first item on the agenda is whether to sue you, personally, for those ten million euros - and under your country's [[corporate law]], they are allowed to.

## The chain from EU directive to personal claim

Article 20(1) of the NIS2 Directive states that management bodies "can be held liable for infringements" of Article 21. Every EU country's corporate law gives the company a mechanism to recover [[damages]] from a director who breached a [[statutory duty]] - something the law makes you personally do, different from an ordinary business decision.

When a NIS2 fine or a cyber incident causes financial loss, the company can sue you personally for those damages. The claim comes from your own organisation - through the [[shareholders]] or the supervisory board - not from the regulator. Lesson 1.3 covers the regulator's powers (fines and the Article 34 manager ban). This lesson covers the separate exposure from your own company.

## The reverse burden of proof

In many EU countries, including Germany, the [[reverse burden of proof]] applies to director liability. The company does not have to prove you were careless. You have to prove you were careful.

The dated [[sign-off]], the training record, the meeting minutes, the [[risk register]] with your signature - those are the evidence you use to prove [[due care]]. Without them, you are arguing from an empty file.

## What to verify this week

Ask your legal counsel: "Under our national corporate law, what is the standard of care I am held to as a director, and does the burden of proof run against me?"

## Key takeaways

1. The EU directive creates the duty; your national corporate law turns it into a personal financial claim your own company can bring against you.
2. In many EU countries - including Germany - the burden of proof runs against the director: you must prove due care, not the other way around.
3. Documentation is your only defence - the dated sign-off, the training record, the meeting minutes, and the risk register with your signature.
