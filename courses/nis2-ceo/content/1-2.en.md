# Lesson 1.2: Who Is in Scope - Essential vs Important Entities

Most CEOs assume their company either is or is not covered by NIS2. The reality is more precise. The directive creates two categories of covered entities - Essential and Important - and the category you fall into determines how aggressively the regulator can come after you.

## What the rule means

Article 3 of the NIS2 Directive sorts covered companies into two lists. [[Annex]] I is the Essential list - energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructure, health, drinking water, wastewater, digital infrastructure, ICT service management, public administration, and space. Annex II is the Important list - postal and courier services, waste management, chemicals, food production and distribution, manufacturing, digital providers, and research organisations.

Most companies enter scope through a simple [[size threshold]]: fifty or more employees, or more than ten million euros in annual [[turnover]]. If you meet either [[criterion]] and operate in a listed [[sector]], you are in. A few narrow categories - DNS providers, top-level domain registries, and certain electronic communications providers - are included regardless of size. Member states can add sectors on top of the EU list.

For scale: Germany's federal cybersecurity authority (the BSI) estimates roughly 29,500 entities in scope in Germany alone. It is the most concrete number any EU regulator has published.

## Why you care

If you are classified as an [[Essential entity]], the regulator can audit you whenever they choose - no reason, no incident, no complaint needed. The directive calls this [[proactive supervision]] under Article 32. If you are classified as an [[Important entity]], the regulator only audits you when something triggers it - an incident, a complaint, a report. The directive calls this reactive supervision under Article 33.

The penalty ranges differ between the two categories - Lesson 1.3 covers those in detail. The substantive obligations under Article 21, however, apply equally to both. Essential and Important entities must implement the same ten measures. The difference is [[enforcement]] intensity, not [[compliance scope]].

## What to ask your legal team

"Have we completed a [[scoping assessment]], and which category are we in?" If the answer is "we have not checked," that is the first action item. Everything in this course that follows depends on whether you are in scope - and if you are, whether you sit in the Essential or the Important category.

## Key takeaways

1. NIS2 covers two categories - Essential (Annex I sectors) and Important (Annex II sectors) - with a size threshold of fifty employees or ten million euros in annual turnover for both.
2. Essential entities face proactive supervision: the regulator can audit without cause. Important entities face reactive supervision: the regulator audits only when something triggers it.
3. The ten measures under Article 21 apply equally to both categories - the difference is enforcement intensity, not the scope of what you must implement.
