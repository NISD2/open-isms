# Lesson 2.14: Measure 9 - HR Security, Access Control, and Asset Management

Most CEOs think of HR and IT as two separate departments. Under NIS2 they are one compliance system. The directive names them in the same clause - on purpose - because when they do not talk to each other, former employees keep their access for weeks after leaving - exactly the structural break the joiner-mover-leaver process is designed to prevent.

## What this measure means

Article 21(2)(i) of the NIS2 Directive requires "human resources security, access control policies and asset management." Three duties bundled together because they only work as a system.

HR security covers how people join and leave. The critical part is offboarding - when someone leaves, access must be revoked within hours, not days or weeks. For critical roles, revocation should happen the moment the termination is decided, not the day the person walks out. The process that connects HR and IT across the full employee lifecycle - joining, changing roles, and leaving - is called the [[joiner-mover-leaver]] process. If any of those three handovers is broken, Measure 9 is broken.

Access control means every user has only the permissions they need for their job, permissions are assigned by role rather than individually, and the full list is reviewed at least annually. On top of that, [[privileged accounts]] - administrators, root access, domain administrators - need their own separate policy with stronger controls. The CIR (Annex 11.3) requires this as a dedicated policy, already listed among the eleven required policies in Lesson 2.6. Every asset on the inventory (Lesson 2.2) should have an owner and a current list of who can access it.

## What happens if you don't have it

The audit probes this measure at the leaver step because that is where the HR-IT handover structurally breaks. A former employee still holding a live VPN login or an active administrator account weeks after departure is the precise finding the measure is meant to prevent. The underlying failure is always the same: HR knew the person left, IT did not, and nobody checked. Privileged accounts make the exposure worse - an orphaned admin account is not just access, it is full control.

## What to ask your team

Two questions. Ask IT and HR together: "How long does it take to revoke all access when someone leaves, and does HR notify IT the moment the decision is made or the day the person leaves?" If the answer is vague, the process is not documented. Then: "When was the last time we reviewed the full list of who has access to what - and when was the last time we reviewed the [[privileged accounts]] list specifically?" If either review has not happened in the last year, that is your first action item.

## Key takeaways

1. Article 21(2)(i) bundles HR security, access control, and asset management because they are one system - a person who left and still has access is a failure of all three.
2. The leaver step is the structural break point of Measure 9: access must be revoked within hours, not days, for critical roles.
3. Privileged accounts need their own dedicated policy and their own review cadence, as required by CIR Annex 11.3.
