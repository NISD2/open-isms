# Lesson 1.9: The Ten Measures - Overview

There are ten of them. Not eight. Not twelve. Ten specific categories of measures the law says your company must have in place - and the regulator walks into an audit with a checklist of exactly those ten. Having eight out of ten is not a passing grade.

## The list in Article 21

Article 21(2) of the NIS2 Directive lists ten categories of cybersecurity risk-management measures. They group into four blocks:

**Foundation:** (a) [[risk analysis]] and information security policies, and (f) effectiveness testing.

**Incident and continuity:** (b) [[incident handling]] and (c) [[business continuity]] - backups, recovery, and crisis management.

**Supply chain and systems:** (d) [[supply chain]] security and (e) security in how you buy, build, and maintain IT systems, including [[vulnerability]] handling.

**People and access:** (g) [[cyber hygiene]] and staff training, (h) [[cryptography]] policies, (i) human resources security, [[access control]], and [[asset management]], and (j) [[multi-factor authentication]] and secured communications.

Each category needs three layers: technical, operational, and organisational. Your CISO will call these [[Technical and organisational measures|technical and organisational measures]], or TOMs. A firewall without a policy is incomplete. A policy without a working process is incomplete.

## Proportionality - scaled to your size, not to zero

[[Proportionality]] in Article 21(1) scales depth to your size, sector, and risk exposure. It does not eliminate any category. A fifty-person company is not expected to run a Fortune 500 security operations centre - but it must have all ten at a level that matches its situation.

The common failure: most measures in good shape, one or two missing entirely. The missing ones are the violation. Module 2 covers each in detail.

## What to ask your CISO this week

"Can you show me, for each of the ten Article 21 categories, a documented policy, a working procedure, and evidence that we are actually doing it?"

## Key takeaways

1. There are ten specific categories in Article 21(2) - all ten apply to every covered entity, none are optional, and the regulator audits against all of them.
2. "Proportionate" means scaled to your size and risk exposure - not reduced to zero; a small company still needs all ten categories at its scale.
3. The common failure mode is most measures in good shape but one or two missing - the missing ones are the violation.
