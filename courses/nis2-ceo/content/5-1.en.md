# Lesson 5.1: Your First 90 Days After This Course

You have finished forty-four lessons. You understand the law, the penalties, the ten measures, and the eleven decisions that stay on your desk. Understanding is not compliance. This lesson is the order of operations - the sequenced plan that turns knowledge into a [[defensible]] [[baseline]] within ninety days.

In its curriculum for management training (October 2026), the BSI recommends taking seven decisions after the training, ideally within two to six weeks: confirm the scope, set the responsibilities, approve a register of measures, adopt rules for accepted risks and escalation thresholds, approve the reporting and crisis process, set up a regular report to management, and plan the follow-up training. All seven are in this plan. Six of them fall in the first four weeks; the rules for accepted risks follow in month 2, once the risk register exists.

## Week 1-2: Confirm scope and register

Three things before anything else.

Confirm your scoping assessment - Essential or Important. The classification determines your supervision regime, reporting deadlines, and penalty ceiling. Get it wrong and every decision downstream rests on the wrong assumption. While you are at it, confirm or correct the scope: which services, which IT and OT, which subsidiaries, which group IT and which critical service providers are part of it (Lesson 1.2).

If you have not registered with the BSI, begin immediately. Late registration is worse than imperfect registration. File now, correct later.

Confirm the CISO reporting line. If there is a layer between your CISO and the board, start restructuring this week. Everything in the next eighty days depends on one person who can brief you directly. At the same time, decide who in management is responsible for cybersecurity and which decisions the whole board takes (Lesson 1.5).

## Week 3-4: Governance foundation

Sign the top-level information security policy - or trigger a re-sign-off if the current version is older than twelve months. Unsigned or stale, nothing underneath holds weight.

Establish the monthly CISO briefing. Recurring thirty-minute meeting: current risk posture, open incidents, progress on the measures, anything that needs your decision.

Confirm the policies Article 21 asks for exist. Your CISO produces the list, typically: risk management, incident response, business continuity, supplier security, access control, cryptography, asset management, HR security, vulnerability handling, effectiveness evaluation, and the top-level policy. How many separate documents that takes is up to you, unless you are one of the digital providers the CIR covers (Lesson 2.6). Record this in a register of measures, each with priority, owner, deadline, budget need and evidence, and approve it.

Approve the reporting and crisis process: who releases the early warning, who deputises for whom, and how everyone can be reached (Lesson 1.11). Set up the decision log (Lesson 3.2), and put the follow-up training in the calendar.

## Month 2: Risk assessment

Commission or review the asset inventory. Every system, application, data store, and supplier your business depends on must appear in a register. If none exists, your CISO builds it. If one exists but is older than a year, schedule the review.

Commission or review the risk management methodology document. This is the scoring scales, the asset-based-or-not decision, the acceptance criteria, the review cadence. Auditors ask for it separately from the register, because it is the document that keeps the register consistent over time and across teams.

Commission or review the risk register. The asset inventory feeds directly into the risk assessment - every asset maps to threats, vulnerabilities, and impact ratings.

Sign the [[residual risk acceptance]]. The [[artefact]] most CEOs miss and that every audit explicitly asks for. Your signature proves you personally reviewed what the company decided to live with. Without it, the risk assessment is unsigned work product. Set your escalation thresholds at the same time: the score at which a risk comes to you (Lesson 2.3).

## Month 3: Controls and readiness

Walk the ten measures with your CISO. One question per measure: can you show me the evidence? Not a description - the actual document, configuration, or record. Where the answer is "not yet," that is a remediation item with a deadline.

Test the incident response plan. Run a [[tabletop exercise]] - a structured walkthrough of a realistic scenario with the people who would respond. A one-hour session with documented minutes is worth more than a hundred-page plan never tested.

Review supplier contracts for three clauses: security requirements, incident notification obligations, and audit rights. Flag gaps in your top ten suppliers for renegotiation.

Schedule the annual [[management review]]. BSI-Standard 200-1 section 7.4 requires it - minutes and a management body signature. If it is not in the calendar, it will not happen.

## Ongoing after 90 days

The ninety-day plan builds the baseline. The [[cadence]] maintains it.

Monthly: CISO briefing. Thirty minutes, standing agenda, documented.

Annually: policy re-sign-off, risk register review with updated residual risk acceptance, management review with minutes and signature.

Regularly, at an interval set by your risk exposure: management body training refresher. The BSI names as follow-up formats an annual status report, training prompted by major changes (Lesson 1.6), and separate crisis team exercises, kept apart from the training.

Compliance is not a project. The baseline is the foundation. The cadence keeps it defensible.

## Key takeaways

1. The first two weeks are about scope, registration, and the CISO reporting line - these are the prerequisites everything else depends on.
2. By day ninety, you should have signed the top-level policy, the risk register with residual risk acceptance, and walked the ten measures with evidence - those three artefacts carry the most audit weight.
3. Compliance is not a project - the ninety-day plan builds the baseline; the monthly and annual cadence maintains it.
4. The BSI recommends seven decisions after the training. This plan contains all seven, six of them in the first four weeks.
