# Lesson 2.4: Why Risk Management Is Continuous

When was the last time your risk register was updated? If the answer is "we did one last year when we started the NIS2 project," the register is already out of date - and under Article 21, an out-of-date register is a non-compliant register.

## What the rule means

Article 21(1) of the NIS2 Directive does not say "conduct a risk analysis." It says you must "take appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures to manage the risks" - present tense, continuously, every day the company exists. Risk management is a cycle, not a project: identify, analyse, treat, monitor, and back to identify. The cycle never stops because the inputs change faster than an annual review can keep up with - threats evolve, assets change, business context shifts, and what you were willing to accept two years ago may be outside your risk appetite today.

The minimum review cadence is annual. BSI Standard 200-1, Section 7.4 hardcodes this as the only mandatory annual frequency in the framework - the formal [[management review]] where you, the management body, sit down and assess whether the whole security programme still works. That review is a dated, signed protocol - a mandatory document in any audit.

## Why you care

On top of the annual review, the register has to be re-scored whenever a [[trigger event|trigger]] fires. The triggers are practical: a major incident at your company or a peer, a significant business change like an acquisition or new supplier, a new vulnerability disclosure affecting software you run, a regulatory change, or an audit finding that names a risk you had not tracked. If any of these happened and the register was not updated, the register is stale - and the auditor will notice, because they compare the "last reviewed" date against your known business changes.

The next ten lessons cover the ten controls NIS2 requires. Each one exists to move a risk out of the top-right corner of the matrix. But the controls only work if the matrix underneath them stays current.

## What to ask your team

Two questions. "When was the risk register last updated, and what triggered the update?" If the answer points to the initial NIS2 project and nothing since, the register is stale. "What is the process for triggering a re-score when something changes?" If there is no documented trigger process, the [[risk management cycle]] is not running.

## Key takeaways

1. Article 21 is a continuous duty - a risk register is a living document, and a frozen register is a non-compliant register.
2. The minimum review cadence is annual (BSI Standard 200-1, Section 7.4), but any major incident, business change, vulnerability disclosure, or audit finding triggers a re-score on top of it.
3. The auditor compares the "last reviewed" date against your known business changes - if the date is older than the last change, the register is already a finding.
