# Lesson 1.10: All-Hazards Approach and State of the Art

Most CEOs think cybersecurity is a digital problem. Article 21 disagrees. A server room fire, a contractor who walks out with a laptop, a flood that takes out the backup power - under NIS2, every one of those is a cybersecurity risk-management failure. Two phrases in Article 21(1) expand the scope of what you are responsible for: "all-hazards" and "state of the art."

## All-hazards: everything that can hurt the systems

Article 21(1) requires risk management "based on an [[all-hazards approach]] that aims to protect network and information systems and the [[physical environment]] of those systems from incidents."

You must protect the IT systems - and the rooms they sit in - from every threat, not just hackers. Cyber attacks, physical break-ins, fires, floods, power outages, supplier failures, [[insider threat|insider threats]], and human error are all in scope. If your [[risk assessment]] only lists cyber attacks, it does not meet the all-hazards standard.

## State of the art: the moving yardstick

Article 21(1) requires measures that "take into account the [[state of the art]]." State of the art means current best practice as recognised by [[published standards]] - what a reasonable expert would do right now.

The auditor judges against the standards current at the time of audit, not when you first implemented the [[controls]]. A company that built controls three years ago and has not reviewed them since is not compliant, even if those controls were sufficient when deployed.

Your controls need a [[review cycle]]. At minimum annually, and whenever relevant standards or your business change significantly.

## What to ask your security team

Two questions. "Does our risk assessment include physical and environmental threats, or only cyber attacks?" And: "When did we last review our controls against the current version of the standards we claim to follow?" If the answer to the second is more than a year ago, the controls may no longer reflect the state of the art.

## Key takeaways

1. All-hazards means everything that can hit the systems - cyber attacks, physical threats, environmental events, supplier failures, insider threats, and human error - all in scope under Article 21.
2. State of the art is measured against published standards current at the time of audit, not when you first implemented.
3. Your controls need a review cycle: the rule is not "implement once" but "keep current."
