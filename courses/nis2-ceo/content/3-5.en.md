# Lesson 3.5: What You Cannot Delegate - The Checklist

Eleven decisions. Eleven signed [[artefact|artefacts]]. The audit that decides whether you did your job under NIS2 is a check against that list - and the single artefact most CEOs cannot produce is the one that proves you personally accepted the risks the company decided to live with.

## The non-delegable checklist

Article 20(1) of the NIS2 Directive states: "Member States shall ensure that the management bodies of essential and important entities approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures... and oversee its implementation." There is a set of decisions the law has kept on your desk. They cannot be pushed down to the CISO, the legal department, or an external consultant. The management body decides what and why. The CISO decides how. When the line is blurry, treat the decision as [[non-delegable duty|non-delegable]] and sign it yourself.

Here is the [[consolidated]] list:

One: approval of the cybersecurity risk-management measures - the top-level security policy, signed in your name.

Two: formal acceptance of [[residual risk|residual risks]] - the risks the company has decided to live with. The EU implementing regulation requires risk assessment results and residual risks to be accepted by the management body. This is the one most CEOs miss.

Three: approval of the security budget.

Four: crisis declaration during a major incident - formally activating the company's crisis procedures.

Five: the Article 23 reporting decision. Your team drafts, you approve.

Six: customer notification decisions under Article 36. Your team drafts, you approve.

Seven: authorisation of any extraordinary payment - ransomware, settlement, or emergency vendor engagement.

Eight: approval of all public statements during a crisis.

Nine: engagement of external [[forensics]] or legal counsel in major incidents.

Ten: resolution of conflicts between security and other business priorities when they [[escalate]] to the board.

Eleven: the annual management review of the whole security programme - a formal documented review, with [[minutes]], signed by the management body, at least once a year.

## The artefact most CEOs miss

The [[residual risk acceptance]] is the single document auditors look for first and the single document most CEOs cannot produce. Every risk your team identifies and chooses not to fully [[mitigated|mitigate]] becomes a residual risk. Each one needs to appear in the signed [[risk register]] with a rating, a description of what is being accepted, and a named owner with a review date. Your signature on that consolidated register is what proves you personally reviewed what the company decided to live with.

When your CISO presents the risk register, ask three things: are the residual risks listed individually, each with a rating and the reason for not fully mitigating? Does each one have a named owner and a review date? Does the list cover non-technical risks too - people, processes, suppliers, physical locations - not just IT systems? If the answers are vague, do not sign.

The absence of the signature is the [[finding]]. If the company has an incident in an area you chose to live with, the auditor will ask for the document that proves you personally accepted that risk.

## Key takeaways

1. There are eleven non-delegable decisions under NIS2 - eleven artefacts, every one of which an auditor will ask for.
2. The single one most CEOs miss is the residual risk acceptance - your signed acknowledgement of the risks the company decided to live with.
3. The rule for borderline decisions: if you are unsure whether it is your signature that is needed, treat it as non-delegable and sign it yourself.
