# Lesson 3.7: What Happens During a Regulator Audit

The BSI sends a letter announcing an [[on-site inspection]]. This lesson walks you through the process from trigger to close-out so nothing on that day is a surprise.

## What the rule means

The NIS2 Directive splits supervision into two regimes. Article 32 governs Essential entities: the regulator can audit you proactively, at any time, without cause. No incident required. No warning owed. Article 33 governs Important entities: the regulator audits reactively - triggered by an incident, a complaint, intelligence from another authority, or a random selection from the registry. Both regimes apply the same ten-measure checklist from Article 21.

## What happens

A typical audit runs three to five days on-site. Article 32(4) gives the auditor the power to request any document, conduct on-site inspections, and [[interview]] anyone in the company - including you personally.

The auditor requests your document set in advance. On-site, they interview the CISO, the [[compliance officer]], and you. They will ask you to explain your risk methodology, walk through a recent incident, and show how [[remediation]] is tracked. They inspect systems to cross-check what the documents say against what they find. If your policy says you encrypt data at rest and the auditor finds unencrypted databases, that is a [[finding]].

Seven documents form the backbone of your [[audit file]]: your Article 21 policy set, the signed risk register with residual risk acceptance, incident reports and lessons learned, training records (yours and your staff's), CISO-to-board reporting minutes, the asset inventory, and supplier contracts with the three critical clauses.

## What the auditor's report contains

The report contains three categories. A finding is a [[non-conformance]]: something the law requires that you do not have or do not do. It triggers a [[remediation plan|remediation order]] with a deadline. An [[observation]] is an area that falls below good practice but does not violate the law. It does not trigger an order. The distinction matters: findings carry legal consequences, observations do not.

## How to respond

Acknowledge every finding. Produce a remediation plan: named owner, deadline, corrective action. The auditor returns within three to six months to verify. If remediation is incomplete, the regulator escalates: binding instructions under Article 32(5), public disclosure, or fines.

Good faith matters. A company with an honest gap and a credible plan is treated differently from one that fabricates records. Cooperation is not optional - Article 32(4) makes obstruction itself a violation - but the quality of your cooperation shapes every discretionary decision the regulator makes.

## What to ask your team

"If the BSI called tomorrow, could we produce every document on this list within twenty-four hours?" If the answer is no, that is your preparation gap.

## Key takeaways

1. Essential entities can be audited at any time without cause; Important entities are audited when triggered - both face the same ten-measure checklist from Article 21.
2. The audit is a document review and interview process - the seven documents above are the backbone of your audit file.
3. Findings must be remediated within the ordered timeline; good-faith cooperation is the single strongest factor in how the regulator treats you.
