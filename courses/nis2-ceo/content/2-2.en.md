# Lesson 2.2: What Is an Asset?

In 2017, Equifax had a patch for a critical vulnerability. They applied it to every server except one. That one server was not on any list. One hundred and forty-seven million records were stolen through it. The root cause was not the missing patch - it was the missing inventory entry. This lesson covers what "[[asset]]" means under NIS2 and why the inventory of those assets is the foundation that every other security control sits on.

## What the rule means

Article 21(2)(i) of the NIS2 Directive names asset management as one of the ten measures you are personally responsible for. The reason it is on the list is simple: every other control - patching, access control, encryption, backup (potentially more) - assumes someone has a complete list of the things being protected. Without that list, controls are applied to whatever someone remembers, and whatever they forget is where the breach happens.

An asset, in compliance language, is anything your business depends on to operate. Not just servers and laptops. Your customer database, your cloud subscriptions, your buildings, your suppliers, the employees who hold knowledge nobody else has, and the business processes that keep revenue flowing. If losing it would hurt the business, it is an asset. The full list of them is called the [[asset inventory]], and it must be kept current - an inventory that was correct last year and has not been updated is already a gap.

## Why you care

The Equifax case is the clearest example: the patch existed, the team was capable, and the process was in place. The asset inventory was incomplete, and the incomplete entry is what turned a routine patching cycle into a breach that cost over a billion dollars. An auditor cannot evaluate any other measure without this inventory in hand, because every other control is only as complete as the list it covers. Lesson 2.14 covers asset management as a structured obligation - what to include, how to classify, and how to keep it current. This lesson is about the concept: if the inventory is wrong, nothing built on top of it is reliable.

## What to ask your team

One question: "Show me the asset inventory. When was it last updated, and does it include cloud subscriptions and contractor access?" If the answer covers only servers and laptops, the inventory is incomplete. If the last-updated date is more than six months old, the inventory is drifting.

## Key takeaways

1. An asset is anything the business depends on - people, processes, cloud subscriptions, and buildings, not just IT hardware.
2. The asset inventory is the foundation every other security control sits on; if the list is incomplete, the controls built on it are incomplete.
3. The inventory comes before everything else - policies, the risk matrix, and every other measure are built on top of it.
