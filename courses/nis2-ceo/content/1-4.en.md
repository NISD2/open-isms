# Lesson 1.4: The Registration Obligation

Your company is covered by NIS2. You have three months to tell the regulator who you are. If you miss that window, the first thing the regulator sees when they pull up your file is a missing [[registration]] - and that, by itself, is a separate fine. Most leadership teams think of the ten security measures in Article 21 and forget there is a standalone administrative duty that has to be done first.

## What Article 27 requires

Article 27 of the NIS2 Directive requires covered companies to register with their national [[competent authority]] within three months of becoming subject to NIS2. The registration must include the company name, address, sector, contact details - including a twenty-four-seven [[incident contact]] - the services the company provides, the EU countries where those services are offered, and, for digital infrastructure providers, IP address ranges.

Late or incorrect registration is sanctioned separately under Article 32. It is its own violation. It does not require a cyber incident to trigger it. Registration is the first thing the regulator checks when they start any audit - a binary question: either you are on their list or you are not. There is no grey area. The registration is also how the regulator knows which contact to call when there is a sector-wide incident. Without it, they cannot reach you - and that communication gap is itself a [[compliance gap]].

## The compliance picture

As of March 2026, roughly eleven thousand five hundred of an estimated twenty-nine thousand five hundred in-scope German companies had registered by the deadline. The remaining eighteen thousand are each one regulator query away from their first NIS2 fine.

The practical steps once your counsel confirms you are covered: open the registration [[portal]] for your country, confirm which [[credential|credentials]] you need and request them immediately - credential requests alone can take weeks. Assemble the registration data: [[sector]], services, EU [[footprint]], contact details. The one item that usually does not exist yet is the twenty-four-seven incident contact - decide who that person is and document who covers them when they are unavailable. Personally review the submission before it goes in. If the data is wrong, the regulator treats that as incorrect registration, which is its own fine. Set a reminder for the update duty - if any registered information changes, you must update the registration. This is not a one-time [[filing]].

## What to ask your team

"Are we registered with the competent authority under NIS2, and when was the registration last reviewed for accuracy?" If the answer is "we have not registered," that is a compliance gap that exists right now, independently of everything else.

## Key takeaways

1. Article 27 gives you three months from the day your company becomes covered to register with the national regulator - and the registration must include a twenty-four-seven incident contact.
2. Missing or incorrect registration is a separate fine under Article 32, independent of any cyber incident - and it is the easiest violation for the regulator to detect.
3. In Germany, only about eleven thousand five hundred of an estimated twenty-nine thousand five hundred in-scope companies had registered by March 2026 - the rest are exposed to immediate enforcement.
