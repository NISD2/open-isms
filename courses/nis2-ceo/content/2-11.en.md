# Lesson 2.11: Measure 6 - Effectiveness Assessment

Most CEOs think Measure 6 is about running audits. It is not. It is about one specific obligation: to sit down once a year, review whether your entire security programme is actually working, and sign a document that says what you decided. BSI Standard 200-1, Section 7.4 hardcodes this as the only mandatory annual frequency in the framework. Without that document, Measure 6 fails the audit outright - no matter how good your KPIs look.

## What this measure means

Article 21(2)(f) of the NIS2 Directive requires "policies and procedures to assess the effectiveness of cybersecurity risk-management measures." You have to measure whether the controls you signed off on are actually doing the job, and you have to review those measurements formally.

Your CISO uses two terms for the measurement part. [[KPI|KPIs]] - Key Performance Indicators - measure how well a process is running (for example, what percentage of patches were applied on time). [[KRI|KRIs]] - Key Risk Indicators - measure how much risk you are currently carrying (for example, how many critical vulnerabilities are open right now). These numbers feed into a formal annual meeting called the [[management review]].

## What happens if you don't have it

The management review is a dated, signed document - the [[management review protocol]] - recording what was reviewed, what was decided, and who owns each follow-up action. It covers the status of the programme since the last review, whether previous decisions were carried out, new threats, resource needs, and any residual risks to accept (tying back to Lesson 2.3).

What the audit actually demands for Measure 6 is not "show me your KPIs." It is "show me the protocol of your last annual management review, and show me that the follow-up actions from the previous year were closed." If that protocol does not exist, the whole of Measure 6 fails the audit regardless of how good the numbers are. The CIR (Annex 7.1.1) requires an effectiveness assessment policy, making this a documented obligation, not a suggestion.

## What to ask your team

Two questions. "What date is the annual management review, and what is on the agenda?" If the answer is vague, set the date yourself within six weeks. "Can you show me last year's protocol and which follow-up actions were closed?" If neither exists, that is the first action item - not the KPI dashboard, the protocol.

## Key takeaways

1. Measure 6 is not "run some audits" - it is a written discipline of measuring effectiveness through KPIs and KRIs and reviewing them formally once a year.
2. The annual management review is a discrete obligation under BSI Standard 200-1, Section 7.4; it must produce a signed protocol and close out last year's follow-up actions.
3. The auditor tests this measure by asking for the protocol, not the KPIs - a review without a dated, signed document is a review that never happened.
