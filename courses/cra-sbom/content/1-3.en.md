# Lesson 1.3: The 10-Year Retention Rule and Who Gets Access

The CRA does not just require you to create an SBOM. It requires you to keep it. This lesson covers the retention period, where the SBOM fits in the broader [[technical documentation]] package, and who can request it and under what conditions.

## Where the retention rule comes from

CRA Article 31 and Annex VII set out the [[technical documentation]] a manufacturer must compile. The SBOM is one item in that package. Article 31(3) sets the retention period:

> "The manufacturer shall keep the technical documentation and, where applicable, the EU declaration of conformity at the disposal of the national competent authorities for a period of 10 years after the product with digital elements has been placed on the market, or for the support period whichever is longer."

Two numbers to hold onto:

- **10 years** from the date the product was placed on the market (the date it was first made available to customers in the EU)
- Or the **[[support period]]**, whichever is longer

The support period under CRA Annex I Part I requires manufacturers to provide security updates for at least five years, or for the expected product lifetime if that is shorter. In most cases, 10 years will be the longer number. But for long-lived industrial products, embedded controllers in machinery expected to run for 20 years, the support period may exceed 10 years.

## What the technical documentation package contains

The SBOM is one item in a larger set of documents you must maintain:

1. **General description** of the product, its intended purpose, and its version
2. **Design and development documentation** including architecture, design decisions, threat model, and [[coordinated vulnerability disclosure]] policy
3. **Risk assessment** — the cybersecurity risk assessment required by CRA Annex I Part I
4. **SBOM** — the machine-readable component inventory
5. **Secure development documentation** — coding standards, security testing, and testing methodology
6. **Test results** — results of security testing including penetration tests or security evaluations
7. **Vulnerability management documentation** — the process for monitoring, handling, and disclosing vulnerabilities
8. **EU declaration of conformity** — the document the manufacturer signs confirming the product meets CRA requirements

The SBOM must be kept in sync with the product. When you release a new version that changes the component inventory, the SBOM in the technical documentation package must be updated. A two-year-old SBOM for a product that has had twelve security patch releases is not compliant documentation.

## Who can request the SBOM

The CRA creates a two-tier access model for the SBOM.

### Market surveillance authorities

Article 31(3) requires you to keep the technical documentation "at the disposal of the national competent authorities." A [[market surveillance authority]], in Germany, the BSI; in France, ANSSI; in the Netherlands, the NCSC, can request your technical documentation, including the SBOM, at any time.

You must provide it within a reasonable timeframe. The regulation does not define "reasonable" for this specific document, but the principle from EU market surveillance law is that you should be able to produce it promptly, days, not weeks.

Article 35 CRA gives market surveillance authorities the power to order a manufacturer to provide the technical documentation if there is evidence the product may not be compliant. Article 36 covers the situation where a product presents a significant cybersecurity risk: authorities can order its withdrawal from the market.

### The public

The SBOM is not a public document under the CRA. Article 13(5) says "draw up" and maintain it, not publish it. The CRA does not require you to make the SBOM available to customers, competitors, or the general public.

Some manufacturers choose to publish their SBOMs voluntarily as a signal of transparency. That is a business decision, not a legal requirement.

### Customers with contractual rights

Business-to-business contracts may give enterprise customers the right to request the SBOM as part of supplier due diligence under their own NIS2 or supply chain risk management obligations. This is a contractual matter, not a CRA obligation on you.

## Practical retention

An SBOM generated from a Docker image or build artifact is a file. Storing it is straightforward. The challenge is:

1. **Linking the SBOM to the specific build** — the SBOM must correspond to the version of the product that is in the market, not a later build. Use the build artifact hash or the product version string as the link.
2. **Keeping it for 10 years across product version history** — if you release 50 patch versions over a product's life, you need 50 SBOMs, not just the latest one. A [[market surveillance authority]] investigating a vulnerability from three years ago wants the SBOM from the version that was in the market at that time.
3. **Keeping the format readable** — CycloneDX 1.6 JSON or SPDX 2.3 JSON will almost certainly be readable in 10 years. A proprietary format from a tool that may not exist in a decade is a risk.

Store SBOMs in version control alongside your releases. Tag them with the product version and the build timestamp. Treat them with the same discipline as your release artifacts.

## Key takeaways

1. CRA Article 31(3) requires technical documentation, including the SBOM, to be retained for 10 years after first placement on the market, or for the support period, whichever is longer.
2. The SBOM is one item in the full technical documentation package. It must be kept current across product versions.
3. Market surveillance authorities can request the technical documentation at any time. You must produce it promptly.
4. The SBOM is not a public document. The CRA does not require you to publish it.
5. Store SBOMs linked to specific product versions. Ten years means retaining the SBOM for every release, not just the current one.
