# Lesson 2.2: The Hot Wash. The Debrief That Saves the Year

The [[hot wash]] is the meeting that happens right after the exercise ends. Same room. Same people. Same day. Twenty minutes is enough.

The hot wash turns a four-hour exercise into a year of improvements. Skip it and the exercise was theatre.

## Why "hot"

"Hot wash" is a term from emergency services. The opposite is a "cold wash," a formal review days or weeks later. Both have a place. The hot wash is the one [[CIR 2024/2690]] Annex Section 3.6 has in mind when it says entities must "carry out post-incident reviews after recovery from incidents" and produce "documented lessons learned." The exercise is a fake incident. The same rule applies.

You run it now, not later, because memory is still fresh. People remember what they thought. They remember what they almost did. They remember the moment things felt wrong. Wait a week and these details fade.

## The four rounds

Run the hot wash in four short rounds. Around the table. Each person speaks in turn.

### Round 1: What went well

One thing each person thinks the team did well. Be specific.

- Good: "We escalated to the CEO within five minutes."
- Bad: "Teamwork was great."

Specific praise builds confidence and tells you what to keep.

### Round 2: What went badly

One thing each person thinks did not work. Without naming a person. Frame it as a process or system gap.

- Good: "The cyber insurance contact was not in the runbook."
- Bad: "Sabine forgot the number."

The first version names a fix. The second names a person and teaches the team not to talk honestly next time.

### Round 3: What surprised us

One thing each person did not expect. A decision that took longer than planned. A piece of information that was missing. An assumption that turned out wrong.

This round is where the most valuable lessons come from. Surprises are the cheap part of any exercise. They are the gaps you would never find by reading a runbook.

### Round 4: What needs to change

The [[facilitator]] collects the items from rounds 2 and 3. Each one becomes an [[improvement item]] with an owner, a deadline, and a link to the relevant Article 21(2) measure. The facilitator writes them into the [[protocol]].

## Three rules for the room

Three rules keep the hot wash useful.

### One: everyone speaks in turn

Nobody dominates. The facilitator enforces this. If one person starts lecturing, the facilitator interrupts and moves to the next person.

### Two: no blame

Findings are about processes, runbooks, training, and tools. Never about a person. Any finding that names a person gets rephrased into the process gap behind it. This is the single most important rule. A team that gets blamed once will not talk honestly at the next exercise.

### Three: every finding becomes a tracked item

A finding that does not become an improvement item did not really come out of the exercise. Owners agree to their deadlines in the room. The facilitator writes everything down before the room clears.

## What goes into each improvement item

Five things per item:

- **What:** one sentence describing the gap and the fix
- **Owner:** a named person, not a department
- **Deadline:** a date, not "soon"
- **Linked rule:** which Article 21(2) sub-point and which §30(2) BSIG paragraph this closes a gap in
- **Done criterion:** what proof will show the item is closed (a document published, a contact added, a test passed)

This is the artefact the auditor checks alongside the protocol. A protocol with no follow-up items is read as theatre.

## Common ways the hot wash fails

- **Skipped entirely** because the exercise ran late and people left
- **Ran as a blame session** instead of the four-round structure
- **Captured but never written up** because the facilitator forgot to put it in the protocol
- **Written up but ignored** because nobody followed up on the deadlines

The facilitator owns all four of these failure modes. Block twenty minutes for the hot wash on the agenda. Run the four rounds. Capture every item. Write the protocol the same day.

## Key takeaways

1. The hot wash runs right after the exercise, in the same room, in about twenty minutes.
2. Four rounds: what went well, what went badly, what surprised us, what needs to change.
3. CIR 2024/2690 Annex 3.6 anchors the post-incident review duty. The exercise is a fake incident, so the same rule applies.
4. Findings are about processes, never about people.
5. Every finding becomes an improvement item with owner, deadline, statutory link, and done criterion.
6. The improvement list feeds the next management review and is part of the audit trail.
