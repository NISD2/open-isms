# Lesson 3.1: Cadence, Final Quiz, and Attestation

You have walked through the law, the people, the facilitator role, one full scenario, the hot wash, and the protocol. This final lesson covers how often to run a tabletop, when to run an extra one, and the eight-question quiz that unlocks your attestation.

## How often to run a tabletop

Once a year is the floor. BSI Standard 200-4 §6.5 says so. ISO 22301:2019 Section 8.6 says the same. The directive itself does not give a number. It uses the [[appropriate and proportionate]] rule in Article 21(1) NIS 2 and the testing rule in CIR 2024/2690 Annex Section 4.1.

In practice, an auditor expects at least one tabletop a year. Less than that and the cadence reads as proof that the incident response and business continuity measures are not being tested seriously.

## When to run an extra one

Run an additional tabletop outside the yearly rhythm when one of these happens:

- **A major IT change.** A new ERP, a new banking platform, a migration to a different cloud, a new way of signing in. The runbook has changed and the team has not practised the new map.
- **A new sector.** You enter a new regulated market that brings new NIS 2 duties or sector-specific reporting rules.
- **A new critical supplier.** You onboard a supplier whose failure would seriously hurt your service. The exercise tests the supplier-incident scenario.
- **A real incident in the recent past.** A real significant incident happened. Six months later, run an exercise on a similar scenario. This tests whether the lessons learned have actually changed how the team behaves.
- **A leadership change.** A new CEO, CISO, or IT manager joins. The exercise teaches the new person the runbook by walking through it.

## Rotate the scenario across years

A team that solves the same scenario every year stops learning. Rotate scenarios so the team builds skill across different incident types.

A simple three-year rotation:

- **Year 1:** A high-impact technical incident (ransomware, supply-chain compromise, OT manipulation).
- **Year 2:** A low-tech but high-stakes incident (CEO fraud, insider data deletion, stolen executive phone).
- **Year 3:** A non-cyber business continuity event (water damage, datacentre outage, long supplier outage).

The Live-System Tabletop library covers all three categories. Pick a different scenario each year and the team's skill set broadens.

## What this attestation does and does not do

The PDF you receive at the end of this course is proof that you completed role-specific cybersecurity training under [[Article 21(2)(g) NIS 2]] for the facilitator role. It does **not** meet the management body training duty under Article 20(2) NIS 2. That duty is met by the management body course on this same platform.

If you are a member of the management body of your company, take both courses. Each one closes a different statutory training duty.

## What the attestation contains

- Your name
- Your company (optional)
- The date you completed the course
- The lesson list with timestamps
- The quiz scores
- The course version
- A legal-basis footer with the Article 21(2)(g) citation
- A QR code linking to a verification page on nisd2.eu

Keep the PDF with your other governance records. When an auditor asks for proof that the person running your annual tabletop is trained, this is the document.

## What to do after the course

Run a tabletop. The Live-System Tabletop module on the nisd2.eu platform has the twelve scenarios as YAML, the runner, the sim-time clock, and the protocol generator. Or self-host the scenarios from the open-source repository on GitHub. Either way, your first exercise is the one that matters most. It shows you the gaps you did not know you had.

## Key takeaways

1. Once a year is the floor. BSI Standard 200-4 §6.5 and ISO 22301:2019 Section 8.6 agree.
2. Extra triggers: major IT change, new sector, new critical supplier, post-real-incident review, leadership change.
3. Rotate the scenario across years so the team builds skill across different incident types.
4. This attestation meets Article 21(2)(g) NIS 2 for the facilitator role. It does not meet Article 20(2) for the management body.
5. The PDF is the document an auditor asks for when checking that the facilitator is trained.
