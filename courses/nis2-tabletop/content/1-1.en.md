# Lesson 1.1: The Article 23 Cascade Your Tabletop Must Rehearse

Every NIS 2 tabletop walks through the same five-step reporting chain. The chain is in Article 23 of the directive. It is the sequence of reports your company must send to the national [[CSIRT]] when a [[significant incident]] happens.

If your tabletop does not walk through this chain, it has missed the most important part.

## What starts the clock

Article 23(1) NIS 2 applies the duty to "any incident that has a significant impact on the provision of their services." [[CIR 2024/2690]] Article 3 defines what counts as significant. Article 4 adds the rule for many small incidents that together cross the line.

The clock starts when you become aware. Not when the alert fires.

An example. Your monitoring system raises an alert at 03:14. The on-call engineer dismisses it as a false positive. At 09:22 the IT manager looks at the logs, sees the same pattern, and tells the CEO: "I think we have ransomware." The clock starts at 09:22, not at 03:14. Becoming aware means a responsible person knows enough to call it significant.

Most teams get this wrong on the first tabletop. They start the clock too early or too late. The exercise teaches them to find the real awareness moment.

## The five reports

Article 23(4) NIS 2 lists five reports. Your tabletop walks through each one.

### Report 1: Early warning within 24 hours

Article 23(4)(a) requires an [[early warning]] within 24 hours of becoming aware. It is short. It tells the CSIRT three things:

- Whether the incident looks like a malicious act
- Whether it might cross borders into other countries
- What you know so far

You are allowed to know very little at this stage. Send the warning anyway.

### Report 2: Incident notification within 72 hours

Article 23(4)(b) requires an [[incident notification]] within 72 hours of becoming aware. This one is longer. It updates the early warning. It includes:

- An initial assessment of how serious the incident is
- Known indicators of compromise
- The impact on your services

### Report 3: Intermediate report on request

Article 23(4)(c) says the CSIRT can ask for an [[intermediate report]] when it wants. No fixed deadline. You answer when asked.

### Report 4: Final report within one month

Article 23(4)(d) requires a [[final report]] within one month of the incident notification. By now you know more. The final report covers:

- A detailed description of the incident
- The type of threat and the root cause
- What you did to fix it and what you are still doing
- Cross-border impact

### Report 5: Progress report if still ongoing

Article 23(4)(e) covers the case where the incident is not yet resolved at the one-month mark. You send a [[progress report]] instead. The final report follows when things are resolved.

## The customer fork

Article 23(2) NIS 2 is a separate duty. When the incident might affect the people who use your services, you must tell them. Without delay. You explain what they can do to protect themselves.

This is its own clock and its own recipients. The German transposition in §35 BSIG (Unterrichtungspflichten) goes further: the BSI can order entities to inform service recipients about the significant incident itself, not only about a threat.

A real tabletop rehearses this fork as a separate decision. Who drafts the customer message. Who signs it off. When it goes out.

## Why the clock is the heart of the tabletop

In a real crisis, the management body's personal liability under Article 20(1) NIS 2 turns on whether the clock was met. A company that detected an incident but missed the 24-hour deadline pays a fine and exposes the CEO personally. The tabletop teaches the team to act on the clock, not on the alert.

Every scenario in the Live-System Tabletop library runs through this chain. The clock is the spine of the exercise.

## Key takeaways

1. The Article 23 reporting chain has five reports: 24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification, intermediate report on request, one-month final report, progress report if still ongoing.
2. The clock starts when you become aware, not when an alert fires.
3. Article 23(2) NIS 2 customer notification is a separate duty with its own clock.
4. CIR 2024/2690 Articles 3 and 4 define "significant."
5. Every NIS 2 tabletop walks through this chain with a visible clock.
