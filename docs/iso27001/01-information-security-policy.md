## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes Kardashev Catalyst UG's commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information: both our own and that of our customers. It provides the framework within which all information security decisions are made.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all information assets, systems, personnel, and suppliers within the ISMS scope defined in NISD2-ISMS-001. It applies to Cory Hisey and Simon Orzel in all their roles, and to all suppliers with access to in-scope systems or data.

---

## 3. Our commitment

Kardashev Catalyst UG is committed to:

- **Protecting customer data**: Our customers trust us with their compliance records, evidence documents, and personal data. We treat that data with the same care we expect our own vendors to apply to ours.
- **Continuous improvement**: Security is not a one-time project. We maintain and improve our ISMS through regular risk assessments, audits, and corrective action.
- **Meeting legal and contractual obligations**: We comply with GDPR as a data processor, and meet the security commitments in our customer DPAs.
- **Being transparent**: We disclose security incidents to affected customers promptly and report to the supervisory authority within GDPR timelines.

---

## 6. Security principles

**Least privilege**: Access is granted only to what is needed for the role, and revoked immediately when no longer needed.

**Defence in depth**: No single control is relied upon. MFA + encrypted storage + network controls + monitoring operate in combination.

**Assume breach**: We design for detection and recovery, not just prevention. Backups are tested. Runbooks exist. Incident response is practiced.

**Security by default**: New systems and features are assessed for security before deployment. Insecure defaults are not acceptable.

**Vendor accountability**: Suppliers with access to data are contractually bound to security standards via DPA. Their compliance is reviewed annually.

Violations of this policy are treated as security incidents and logged. Repeated or deliberate violations will result in access revocation and, where applicable, contractual or legal action.
